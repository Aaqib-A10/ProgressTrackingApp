**Switches.** Every new part has its own switch in the owner console and starts **off for 99 Technologies**, so their screens and notifications do not change on deploy: Requests, People, Quotes and invoices, Report builder, Import, project extras, "Metra speaks first" (alerts, weekly summary, dashboard strip) and Live updates. Every other company has them on. Single sign-on and the database search index have no switch because they change nothing until someone turns them on: single sign-on in Company settings, the database index with `METRA_INDEX`. What 99 Technologies will see: a Single sign-on card in Company settings (admins) and a "Sign in with your company (SSO)" link on the sign-in page.

**24 Nov 2026 changes (Aaqib's review of the live site):**

Three check questions cover it.

- Colour: every number card, the project cards, the person cards, the sidebar's current page and Metra's chat use six gradient tones made from the theme's own colours (`client/src/index.css`, `tone-*` and `g-*`), the same order on every page, light and dark.
- "What everyone is working on" on the dashboards (member: My work; team lead: the team; Super Admin: everyone): what each person has in progress and finished in the period, from project tasks, to-dos and the marketing board, whatever their department (`server/src/lib/teamWork.ts`, `GET /api/dashboard/work`). Another person's private to-dos never show; a project task's title shows only to someone who may open that project. Metra reads it too (`work.people`).
- "Your day" no longer shows an older day's brief.
- Connector sign-in (OAuth 2.1, the MCP way): Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor and other apps connect by pasting `https://<site>/api/mcp`, signing in to Metriq and pressing Allow (`/app/oauth/authorize`); no key to copy. Discovery (RFC 9728, RFC 8414), dynamic client registration and client ID metadata documents, PKCE S256 only, 1-hour access tokens, rotating 90-day refresh tokens (a copied one ends the connection), revocation, and a list with Disconnect on the Connectors page. A connection stops on disconnect, password change, deactivation or a paused company; Metriq support signed in as someone can never connect an app (`server/src/lib/mcpAuth.ts`, 20 tests in `mcpOAuth.integration.test.ts`). Keys in the link still work. Needs once on the server: `sudo bash ~/pulsetrack/scripts/nginx-oauth.sh`.
- Speed: measured, the server answers in milliseconds; the time is the network between Cloudflare and the server (0.5 to 8 seconds a request). Every page's file is now fetched in the background after the app loads, and "who is signed in" is asked while the app's files load. The network itself is the host's (a Cloudflare Tunnel or another host would fix it).

## 1. The short version

- Metriq is already a large product: 153 data tables, 45 API areas, 105 server test files, and a working multi-company core (company walls, owner console, plans, switches, sign-up, billing code).
- It is **built to be sold but not switched on to be sold**. Sign-up is closed, no payment provider is live, and "Forgot password" sends no email in production.
- It is still **shaped like 99 Technologies**. Departments, roles, the Karachi time zone, "Jesse" and Earth Zone are written into shared code. A second company gets a generic department plus custom blocks, not the full product.
- It should **not** try to become Odoo. Every real ERP has accounting and inventory, and payroll is country by country. That is years of work and the crowded part of the market.
- The winnable position: **the team-operations platform for sales, outsourcing and service teams of 10 to 300 people**. Daily reporting, attendance, tasks, chat and calls, CRM and dialer in one place, running in a day with no implementation partner, with an assistant (Metra) that answers from live data under each person's access rules. Those are exactly the points where reviewers attack Odoo, Zoho One, Bitrix24 and ERPNext (section 4).

## 4. What the market expects

**Modules.** Nearly every product compared (Odoo, Zoho One, ERPNext, Bitrix24, NetSuite, Business Central, monday, ClickUp, Keka, BambooHR, Zoho People) has tasks and projects, CRM, HR basics, **approvals and no-code automation**, dashboards, documents, mobile apps and an API or marketplace. The true ERPs add accounting, invoicing and inventory. Payroll is country-limited or a paid add-on everywhere (Zoho Payroll does not cover Pakistan).

**Buyer checklist.** A 2025 analysis of software purchases reports 68% of requests require MFA and SSO in the base plan, audit logs are treated as non-negotiable, and 43% of evaluations ended in disqualification for missing verifiable security credentials ([Software Finder](https://softwarefinder.com/resources/saas-security-report-2025); vendor-adjacent, mostly US, no sample size). Security sign-off is required by 61% of enterprises but 26% of small businesses, so SOC 2 can wait while the basics cannot.

**Pricing.** Three models exist: per user for all apps (Odoo about $9 to $11 on its low-region list, Zoho One $37), flat with a user cap (Bitrix24 $49 / $99 / $199 for 5 / 50 / 100 users), and per site (ERPNext). Metriq's flat $5 / $29 / $99 matches the Bitrix24 model and undercuts it. AI moved in 2026 to credits with overage, and unpredictable AI cost is the most consistent complaint (Gartner warning reported by [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/saas/2026/05/19/sap-customers-warned-ai-agents-could-put-costs-on-autopilot/5242646)).

**AI assistants.** Standard now: chat over the product's own data, drafting and summaries, meeting notes, an agent builder, credit metering. Still rare: answers that respect each person's access across every module, confirm-before-act on real records, flat AI pricing, a connector for outside assistants, and alerts nobody asked for. Metra already has the first four in some form. G2's 2026 review summary lists missing AI among the first dislikes ([G2](https://learn.g2.com/tech-signals-best-erp-software-in-2026-g2-review-insights)).

**Where the incumbents lose.** Odoo: disruptive upgrades, slow support, partner cost. Zoho One: too many apps. Bitrix24: steep learning curve, slow pages. ERPNext: setup and dated interface ([Capterra: Odoo](https://www.capterra.com/p/135618/Odoo/reviews/), [Zoho One](https://www.capterra.com/p/166175/Zoho-One/reviews/), [Bitrix24](https://www.capterra.com/p/113540/Bitrix24/reviews/)).

**Pakistan and South Asia.** Stripe is not open to Pakistan-registered businesses and local wallets are weak at recurring billing (third-party sources). FBR e-invoicing is now mandatory for sales-tax-registered businesses ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1928180/fbr-makes-e-invoicing-mandatory-from-aug-1)). WhatsApp is the default business channel in the region.

## 6. Pricing

- Keep flat plans; they are simple and already undercut Bitrix24.
- Add a plan above Business for 51 to 300 people; today there is no price for that size.
- Offer PKR pricing with bank transfer or local wallet for Pakistan.
- Put "AI included, no credits" on the pricing page.

## 7. Decisions needed from you

1. **Which legal entity sells Metriq?** A Pakistan-registered company cannot use Stripe. A US or UK entity can. Paddle and 2Checkout are alternatives; whether Paddle accepts a Pakistan seller is not verified.
2. **First market: Pakistan and the Gulf, or US small businesses?** It decides WhatsApp and PKR first versus QuickBooks and security paperwork first.
3. **Is 99 Technologies' daily workflow allowed to change during Stage 1?** Departments-as-data touches every ITAD screen. It can be done behind a switch with no visible change, but it is the riskiest step in this plan.

## 8. What I would start now

Stage 0 in this order: 0.1 password-reset email, 0.7 off-server backups, 0.2 two-step sign-in, 0.6 activity log, 0.5 export and delete. These need no decision from section 7 and protect today's users as well as future ones.
